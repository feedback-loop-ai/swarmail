# Re-read a moving subject search during test execution

Twenty trials share each flow's subject. Mailpit and smtp4dev search across recipients, so a neighbour can deliver between pages and change the total. The pager correctly refuses that read. Today the wait ends there and the holder can score a harness loss even when a complete answer is available on the next poll. Issue #388 gives the existing poll wait the rest of its allotted time to obtain that answer.

The governing contract is the accepted reference suite, its wait method, exact matching, measured costs and counted outcomes. This repairs the harness under that contract and requires no new ruling. [Decision 0132 §§7–9 and addenda](../../docs/decisions/0132-mail-comparison-protocol-v4.md)

## Acceptance Criteria

1. In a testexec poll, retry a typed pager `OpError::Incomplete` only for a subject search whose client reports store-wide scope and whose suite runs beside neighbours (parallelism > 1). Begin each attempt at page one on the wait's normal cadence (100 ms in the protocol). At one worker the same refusal immediately yields the original scan operation failure. An unfiltered or recipient-scoped wait retains its existing error handling.
2. A refused attempt contributes zero candidates, foreign sightings, ownership or disappearance evidence. It causes zero fetches, sends, clears or deletes. Only a complete, timely scan can satisfy the count. Exact recipient and subject checks, distinct foreign accounting and fetched identity checks apply to the settled answer.
3. Use one absolute wait cutoff with the existing request tail reserved. An already-expired wait makes zero requests. Every page read and pause fits inside that cutoff, including a retry already in flight. A late wake cannot start another request or deliver a late answer. Sustained refusals exhaust the wait as a counted timeout with a row reason saying the subject listing kept moving. A completed short scan restores the ordinary count diagnostic. Any independent failure, including one following a refused scan, keeps its existing classification. Movement alone supplies no causal evidence for interference.
4. A request-ordinal neighbour delivery inside a two-page search proves the race. With neighbours, prove one delivery, two complete page-request sequences and the exact owned answer. Alone, prove one delivery, one page-request sequence and immediate failure. Continuous movement proves multiple attempts, deadline timeout, the recorded reason and zero client mutations. The parallel recovery proof must fail with the retry disabled. Existing story D tests pass unchanged in behavior.
5. Three ignored Linux dispatcher proofs cover Mailpit p32 and lock-v5 smtp4dev p8 and p32. Local discovery lists each exact name and each isolated missing-prerequisite invocation fails naming its missing executables. A clean host result requires admission of the exact coordinate and bytes, 100/100 scored passes, proved cleanup, measured counters and removed cgroups. Report any other documented outcome verbatim with its denominator, reason, exit and teardown evidence. No exception for these coordinates is established by story B.
6. The eval README records story D's two solo 100/100 runs at eb9e34c (PR #384), lists the three new executions as open, and gives reproducible commands, variables, digest checks and load capture. The reference-suite task ledger tracks the follow-up. Each dispatcher proof runs alone, at most 32 workers and 600 seconds, with output attached to the PR before merge.
